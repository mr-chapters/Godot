# Chapter 5 — Lighting, Landmarks, and Environmental Storytelling

**Mission:** make a map understandable and memorable using its environment, not just signs and text.

## 1. Build a visual hierarchy

Players should quickly distinguish the route, hazards, interactable objects, and background detail. Use size, contrast, silhouette, spacing, and light. If every object is bright and detailed, nothing stands out.

## 2. Landmarks help players form a mental map

A landmark is a recognisable feature that helps players remember locations and directions. It might be a giant tree, a broken bridge, a tall tower, or a distinct colour grouping. Make landmarks visible from useful viewpoints and keep their silhouettes different.

## 3. Tell stories with spaces

Environmental storytelling uses objects and layout to imply what happened. An abandoned workshop with unfinished repairs tells a different story from a spotless laboratory. Give clues through placement, wear, lighting, and what is missing. Avoid making a critical gameplay clue indistinguishable from random decoration.

## 4. Light with purpose

Use light to support the player's attention and mood, but preserve visibility. Test the level at normal movement speed, on a dim display if possible, and with accessibility in mind. Do not rely on a single colour to identify an important path.

## Mini-mission

Create one room that tells a simple story without dialogue. Add three environmental clues and one clear gameplay goal. Ask a friend what they think happened.

## Checkpoint

- What is visual hierarchy?
- How can decoration become confusing?

**Boss challenge:** design a route where the player notices a goal from far away, discovers a story clue on the way, and reaches an optional reward without getting lost.
